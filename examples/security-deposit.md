# Turning a withheld deposit into a clear written request

By the DocBrain team. This walkthrough uses an invented situation, not a customer case or a claim of a successful recovery.

A frustrating situation often arrives as one long message: the lease ended, the keys were handed back, a deposit is still missing, and the landlord has not answered. The useful document is the one that makes the requested action easy to understand.

## Start with the facts you know

Here is a fictional input, separate from the downloadable sample below:

> I moved out of my apartment in Ohio on September 1. My deposit was $900. I returned the keys that day and sent my forwarding address by email. It is now October 8. I have not received the deposit or a list of deductions. I emailed the property manager twice and got no reply. I want the deposit returned, or a written explanation of any deduction.

This story already identifies the location, amount, dates, previous contact and desired result. Add the landlord or management company's name and the rental address if you know them. Do not invent a missing date or a deduction the landlord has not actually claimed.

## What the letter needs to make clear

- Who is writing and which tenancy the request concerns.
- What happened, in a short timeline.
- What has not been received: the money, an explanation, or both.
- What action is requested and where a repayment or reply should go.
- A response deadline and the next available step for the stated location.

An organized letter gives the recipient something concrete to address. It does not turn an uncertain fact into a proven one or guarantee that the recipient will cooperate.
