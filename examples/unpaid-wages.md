# Turning missing pay into a specific payroll request

By the DocBrain team. This is an illustrative walkthrough using invented facts, not a customer testimonial or evidence of wages recovered.

“My final paycheck is wrong” is a real problem, but a payroll contact still needs to know what is missing. A useful written request brings the numbers and the correction into one place.

## Describe the gap

For example:

> I worked 32 hours at an agreed rate of $20 per hour in my last pay period. Those hours are missing from my final paycheck. I am asking for the missing $640. I have my schedule and messages confirming the shifts. I have already emailed payroll but have not received a correction.

This fictional example deliberately leaves out the employer, state and pay dates. Include those details in your own story when you have them. The arithmetic here is simply 32 × $20 = $640; it is not a calculation of penalties, overtime or every possible entitlement.

## Make the request easy to act on

The finished letter should distinguish the agreed rate, hours, amount already paid and amount you believe is missing. It should say which correction you want and ask for a written explanation if the employer disputes the calculation.

It should not invent an employer's admission, a regulator's decision or records you do not possess. Clear facts are more useful than a threatening tone.
